This chapter describes how the lab operates day to day. Norms evolve — if something is unclear or should change, raise it in a meeting or 1:1.

## Scientific philosophy

We study the mental computations that make memories sticky, using direct brain recordings and computational approaches. We value careful experimental design, honest uncertainty, and writing that is clear enough for a smart colleague outside our niche.

Ask questions early. Being stuck silently is more costly than an imperfect draft or a half-formed idea.

## Communication

- Prefer **Slack** for day-to-day updates and quick questions; use email for formal or external correspondence.
- Flag blockers early (data access, compute, clinical scheduling, personal bandwidth).
- Treat clinical collaborators and patients with professional care; never discuss identifiable patient information outside approved channels.

## Meetings and mentorship

- **Lab meetings / journal club:** Attend when scheduled; come ready to discuss papers or your progress.
- **1:1s with Salman:** Use these for project direction, career goals, and feedback — bring an agenda when you can.
- Mentorship is two-way: say what kind of feedback helps you most.

## Authorship, open science, and reproducibility

- Discuss **authorship** early when a project starts or roles change substantially.
- Prefer open science practices: shareable analysis code, clear data provenance, and preprints when appropriate.
- Treat de-identified human intracranial data carefully: follow IRB, HIPAA, and lab data-handling rules (see Technical Skills and clinical collaborators for operational details).

## Hours and professional development

- We care about progress and sustainable work, not performative hours.
- Conferences, coursework, and teaching: plan with Salman so we can support travel and timing.
- Career development (applications, talks, networking) is part of the job — bring it into 1:1s.
